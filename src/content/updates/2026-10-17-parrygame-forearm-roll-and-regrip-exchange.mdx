Internal 0.6.0 adds a first opponent answer to an incomplete palm pin. After the player chooses a bind, the opponent can perform its already committed forearm roll, changing the actual cuff, armed hand and gun orientation until the pin releases. Both solo and paired open training use this exchange, with the same rules in coached and real-time play.

The family-specific roll turns through ninety degrees. The player's left hand follows the pinned material point until release, then visibly travels to a free ready pose. A new aimed Forearm Right action takes a separate 24-tick approach; it establishes control only when the palm arrives inside the declared opportunity. Hand ownership distinguishes held contact, release, approach and established control. Addressing the other opponent must release the previous contact rather than leave an unsupported pin behind.

This is one continuation among alternatives. An early wrist recovery, a contextual step or a vulnerable shot can change the exchange, and the existing quick earned-opening route remains available. In the paired room, the other committed threat continues through the chosen action. Training holds and slow WATCH playback expose the roll, release and regrip decision boundaries without selecting a response for the player. Anatomical callouts show actual hand reach and distinguish a reachable point from an admitted, correctly timed control motion.

The solo probe also makes the regrip's present benefit concrete. Before regripping, the aimed shot lands in one of the three public possible defensive-response scenarios. Completing the regrip raises landing to three of three. At each sampled pre-regrip shot start, the player also survives that shot's 30-tick recovery in all three scenarios. Those are calculated scenario fractions within the stated action horizon, not measured human accuracy or a promise of safety after the next decision. The current solo regrip approach is unopposed by a new lethal commitment, so this result does not establish a balanced opponent or risk in every continuation.

Two new original Blender paired takes bring the library to twenty-one motions per role. The existing nineteen takes in each role retain their original curves. Runtime motion follows the actual hand and weapon frames, including forearm-cuff rotation through a shot interruption or return. Native limb lengths and the original models remain unchanged. The gloves still have no individual finger rig; this milestone does not claim a detailed finger lock, disarm or finished cinematic hand performance.

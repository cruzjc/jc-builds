The next design direction aims for circular positional counterplay. A move that gains control should also change the distance, weapon line or available continuation, creating a different answer for the opponent. Repeating one technique should not remain an easy universal way to win. The intended decisions depend on actual contact, reach, orientation and committed timing, with readable consequences rather than a single prescribed sequence.

Training and the main game are planned to use the same combat mechanics. Stop/go coaching and slow-motion viewing are training assistance; the target main-game rules should preserve the same contact, timing and outcomes without those learning holds. This is a design contract for future main-game work, not a claim that the main game is implemented.

Adjustable opponent AI is also planned so training can offer different levels of resistance while preserving causal commitments and explainable results. The settings and their balance still need implementation and playtesting. Development will retain both 1v1 and 1v2 scenarios to exercise the shared combat system.

The provisional first-release candidate is 1v1. The final release roster remains undecided until the solo exchange earns acceptance; 1v2 stays in development and the future direction rather than becoming a promised first-release requirement. Windows, Steam, controller support and original cinematic choreography remain project goals.

This update records direction only. It adds no combat behavior, AI settings, new animation or game build. Internal 0.5.3 remains the delivered baseline, with its solo-first training, slow WATCH playback, shot estimates and existing validation history unchanged. Human playtesting must establish whether the planned counterplay resists repetitive solutions and remains understandable before release scope is settled.

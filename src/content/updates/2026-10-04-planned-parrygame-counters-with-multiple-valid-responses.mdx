Controls registering in the current player provides useful feedback. The next development gate is a complete, readable combat loop with several valid responses to each attack. This is a design milestone: the delivered Windows player remains 0.3.0, and no new gameplay implementation or build is claimed here.

The planned exchange runs from reading a committed threat to reachable interception and directional control, visible weapon displacement or a bind, then a choice of continuation, recovery, or earned firing. A successful defense creates a decision; it does not automatically award an elimination. Enemy responses must commit before the player's relevant action, and a contested shot can be disrupted before its firing frame.

Four proposed attack families will each need at least two meaningfully different responses: a high firing line, a low firing line, a crossing weapon check, and a close press. They share the existing aim, intercept, left/right/up redirect, contextual retreat, and fire vocabulary. Compromised raised-weapon and crossed-contact positions need readable recovery routes. Original paired Blender motion and bounded IK must show the authoritative contact, displacement, and release without inventing reach.

Development will start with one branching duel, then add weapon motion and recovery, contestable shots, the four readable families, and a compatible overlapping pair. Every opponent uses one shared player state, with actual reach, sight lines, action durations, and body clearance checked together. In real-time play, the survivor resumes pressure until both opponents are eliminated.

Stop/go instruction and spatial callouts will teach both alternatives and recovery before assisted drills and a real-time two-opponent room. The existing guided survivor instruction remains. Implementation, packaged-player acceptance, human readability, and physical controller flow are still ahead; the current automated foundation does not pass this new gate.

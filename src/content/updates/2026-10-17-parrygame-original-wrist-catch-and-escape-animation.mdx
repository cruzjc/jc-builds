Internal 0.8.0 delivers a connected wrist-catch exchange in the Windows development player. The first-release candidate remains focused on one compact solo arena, with detailed original opponents, player hands, weapons and paired animation. Two-opponent encounters remain available as a development scenario.

After an incomplete weapon-arm pin, a catch-capable opponent can reach its free left palm toward the back of the player's controlling wrist. An early recovery or contextual step can make that fixed approach miss. Once caught, holding Intercept and pressing Up rolls the player's own forearm free, then completes the hand's return before another counter. The old pin and the wrist restraint are separate contacts; the right gun hand remains independent. Escape preserves the opponent's committed threat, so another redirect, evade or well-positioned shot must resolve it.

Stop/go training anchors instructions first to the incoming palm and then to the player's actual wrist. The caught-state cards agree on Up, and an offscreen target keeps its explanation while hiding the target ring. WAIT now also stops when a missed catch's hand finishes withdrawing. Reading holds and slow viewing assist learning while full-speed play uses the same combat rules.

The Blender library contains twenty-three original takes per role, preserving all sixty-three earlier editable actions. Source checks and three FBX round trips passed, followed by 2,940 Unity import checks. Six complete catch-related rig routes cover 2,094 dense samples of actual glove, sleeve and cuff skin, native arm lengths, material contact and the forearm roll. Retained motion, footwork, pressure, weapon-return and suspended-animation stages passed separately; this is bounded staged coverage.

Combat validation passed 245 domain checks. Unity integration passed 13,196 synthetic keyboard/mouse, gamepad and interface checks, including ten complete new paths across both transports and the retained routes. The original controller suite passed 135 checks. Synthetic input verifies the transport, while physical controller feel still needs human testing.

The final Direct3D12 Windows player passed twenty-two complete open-training route witnesses, fourteen solo and eight paired, across 560 sampled states. Seven counter lessons passed across 151 states, and the original introduction passed all four reading/practice gates and both eliminations. Separate scene and HUD captures were reviewed. All 334 files in the delivery archive matched the packaged build.

Human playtesting, composited-window readability, motion appeal and performance acceptance remain pending. The second opponent's tested catch stance still leaves shooting openings. Rigid glove fingers, contact occlusion and unfinished sleeve/body continuity also need work. This verified internal delivery does not establish broad balance or final cinematic art, and the focused solo release still needs acceptance.

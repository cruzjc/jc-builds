## Objective

Create an original first-person combat game in which continuous parries, weapon redirects, and contested firing lines reward recognition and positional decisions. A clean hit is usually lethal; the main opportunity to recover comes from regaining control of the exchange.

## How it works

Unity and C# provide the gameplay foundation, with Blender for original asset and animation production. A deterministic combat state graph governs each interception, both fighters' positions, and their available continuations. Shooting is a committed action that an opponent can disrupt before the firing frame. Readable threat cues, concise death explanations, and immediate retries support mastery.

Next art work is shoulder and hem refinement, articulated grasping, contact lighting and complete paired motion. Defeat clipping, elbow creasing, rigid hands and a smooth initial-contact approach remain unfinished.

The broader design direction is circular positional counterplay: a successful move should change the situation and expose a different answer, so repeating one technique is not an easy universal win. Open training already shares its combat mechanics with full-speed play, while slow motion and stop/go assistance belong to training. Practice and Duelist are implemented in both solo and paired development scenarios; broader opponent tuning and adaptive behavior remain planned. The focused first-release candidate would use one compact duel arena and concentrate detail in original character models, first-person hands, weapons and paired animation, with complete contact, release and recovery coverage reviewed by humans in slow and normal motion. Larger content counts are expansion planning. Final art and those wider design goals remain unfinished and unaccepted.

Continued human combat playtesting, physical controller flow, composited-window presentation, broader encounter feasibility, final cinematic content, complete score attack and challenge content, and packaged Windows performance still need acceptance. This internal scene is a production gate; finished-release acceptance remains ahead.
